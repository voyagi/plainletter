Plainletter

Put the letter down. Leave knowing what to do.

A free help desk in a Dutch public library. Someone photographs the letter they cannot read, and the desk reads it back in Dutch and in their own language, with every date and amount marked on the paper it came from.

This page is one of those letters. Everything down this margin is what the desk would say about it.

Marks 4 to 6

Nothing is retyped from memory

Every amount is read off the page, then checked back against it by plain arithmetic rather than by the model that read it. If the two disagree, the whole reading is refused.

Mark 7

One date decides everything

The days left are worked out on a real calendar, Dutch public holidays included, along with the last day a posted objection still arrives in time.

What happens if you do nothing, in a language you read

The broken key, beside Kenteken

When it cannot read something, it says so

The middle character of the number plate is blurred. There is no mark and no number there, and the desk prints ask the visitor to read the number plate rather than a plausible guess.

This is the part that matters most. A wrong deadline at a help desk is worse than no deadline.

Mark 9

Then the route out, with the address on it

Objections, payment plans and appeals go to different places with different windows, so each step carries the official route it came from and the date that route was checked.

Where a sender has not been checked against an official page, the desk says so and sends the visitor to Het Juridisch Loket instead of guessing.

Twelve languages

Both people read the same thing at the same time

Dutch and the visitor language sit at equal width and equal weight, each set in a face drawn for its own script. Numbers, dates and reference numbers keep the letter's own notation in every language, so both readers can point at the same characters on the paper.

The thing that goes home

One sheet, both languages, and a black and white printer

Library printers are monochrome, so nothing on the card carries meaning in colour. Urgency is a word and a treatment, the hatch survives greyscale, and the marks are numbered at the foot against the passages they came from.

A calendar reminder for the deadline goes home with it.

Bring Plainletter to your library

There are 861 Informatiepunt Digitale Overheid desks in the Netherlands, most of them in public libraries. This is built for the volunteers at them and for the people sitting across the table. It is open source, it runs in the EU, and it needs no account to try.
