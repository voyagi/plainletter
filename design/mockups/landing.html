Plainletter

The letter goes home explained.

A visitor brings a letter they cannot read to a library help desk. Ten minutes later they leave with this card: what it is, by when, what happens if they do nothing, and what to do, in Dutch and in their own language.

Every date and every amount on the card is taken from the letter itself and checked before it is printed. Nothing is guessed.

One A4 sheet, printed at the desk, readable in black and white, with a calendar reminder for the deadline.

Built for the desk, not for the app store

The Netherlands runs 861 free help desks in public libraries, the Informatiepunt Digitale Overheid. The people at them are volunteers, not lawyers, and the person across the table is often frightened. Plainletter is made for the two of them together: the volunteer reads the Dutch column, the visitor reads their own.

The hardest minute is the first one. They put the envelope down and ask me if it is bad. Volunteer, library help desk, Rotterdam

What happens to the letter

The reading runs on Amazon Bedrock in Frankfurt. The letter stays inside the European Union, and unless the visitor asks for a case, nothing at all is written down.
